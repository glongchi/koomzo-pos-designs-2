Integration spec · the seam between two modules

The register and the inventory are one ledger

A sale is two events that must never come apart: money in and stock out. The register owns the first, Inventory owns the second, and the entire integration exists to guarantee that a paid ticket always produced a stock movement — and that the movement says which ticket, which till, which operator and what it cost. This document states that seam: the concepts, the business process, the three tiers a shop can run it at, and the gap between design and code that §15 closes.

1 · The one idea

Stock is never edited. It is always moved. There is no screen anywhere in Koomzo that sets an on-hand number directly. Every quantity change — a sale, a delivery, a breakage, a count correction, an opening balance — is a movement row that records what the quantity was before, what changed it, who did it and under what document. On-hand is the sum of movements, never a field someone typed.

That single rule is what makes the register and the inventory the same system rather than two systems that gossip. The register does not "update stock" — it posts a sale movement, in the same ledger, in the same shape, as a warehouse receipt. Which means a shopkeeper asking why do I have 3 left when I bought 12? gets a list of nine answers rather than a shrug.

It is also why the shop can grow without a migration. A market stall that holds no quantities and a pharmacy that tracks batch and expiry are running the same ledger with different numbers of capabilities switched on.

2 · Concepts

The vocabulary the seam depends on. Where the two modules use a different word for one object, both appear.

ConceptWhat it is, and why the register cares
ItemThe thing that exists. Name, SKU, cost, price, unit. Owned by Inventory — the register never creates the truth about an item, though it may create the item itself mid-sale.
Product vs. ItemThe register sells a product: an item plus a channel decision (is it on the grid, in which category, with which tile). One item can be stocked and not sellable; the reverse is also legal.
Show in POSThe single flag that decides whether the register ever sees an item. Off keeps it stockable and countable but off the grid — this is how a salon holds hair colour it never sells, and how a shop hides a line without deleting its history.
LocationWhere stock physically is: store, salon, kiosk, warehouse. Carries a sells flag — a dépôt holds stock but no register. On-hand is always per location; a company-wide number is a report, never a till's answer.
On handUnits at one location right now. A derived figure — the sum of that item's movements there.
MovementOne quantity change at one location: item, delta, before, kind, document, reference, who, cost. The atom of the whole system.
Movement kindsale · receipt · issue · transfer · count · adjust. The register only ever writes sale — and its reversal.
DocumentWhat caused a group of movements: a ticket (SO-), purchase order (PO-), transfer (TR-), count (CC-), adjustment (AJ-). A document is the audit handle — you read the ledger by document, not by row.
ReasonRequired on an adjustment: damaged, expired, sample, back-bar use, recount, theft. This is what turns "we lost 40 000 F of stock" into a shrinkage report an owner can act on.
Unit costWeighted average, re-averaged on every receipt and rounded to the whole franc. The register needs it for exactly one purpose: stamping the cost onto the sale movement so margin is knowable.
Reorder point / parThe level that makes an item "low", and the level to buy back up to. Drives the tile badge at the till and the needs-ordering list.
Batch / lot & serialSub-identities of one item. The register's qualifier chain captures them at the moment of sale, and they travel onto the movement — which is how a pharmacy recalls a lot and a shop honours a warranty.
Composite / recipeAn item whose sale depletes components rather than itself. A gift basket, a cocktail, a plat du jour.

3 · Why a ledger, and not a number

The expensive-looking choice that is actually the cheap one.

It answers "why"

A number tells a shopkeeper they have 3. A ledger tells them they bought 12, sold 7, broke 1, gave 1 as a sample and miscounted 0 — which is the only version that lets them change anything.

It survives staff

Every row carries an operator. In a trade with high turnover and shared devices, "who moved this" is not an accusation — it is the difference between training and guessing.

It makes margin real

Because the sale movement carries the unit cost at the moment of sale, margin is a fact about history, not a recalculation that changes when the next delivery re-averages cost.

It merges offline

Movements are append-only deltas, so two tills that sold offline can both be replayed on reconnect. Two tills that each wrote an absolute on-hand would have to be reconciled by hand — or one would silently win.

The before field earns its place. Every movement stores the on-hand it found. The ledger can then show 12 → 9 rather than a bare −3, which is the difference between a log a shopkeeper can read and one only an accountant can. It also makes a broken sync detectable: if a row's before does not match the running total, something was replayed twice.

4 · A sale, end to end

One ticket for two bottles of shampoo at the Akwa boutique. Money and stock, in order.

Tile tapped→ Read on-hand→ Qualifiers: batch, serial→ Line on ticket→ Tendered & paid→ Sale movement posted→ On-hand 5 → 3→ Low-stock signal
StepWhat happensWhy it is there
Tile tappedThe register resolves the product and asks Inventory for on-hand at this location.A till must never quote a company-wide number. The stock in the dépôt is not sellable at the counter.
Availability shownCount badge when low; dimmed tile and Out at zero; hidden items are not on the grid at all.The register never lies about availability. Selling something absent is worse than not offering it.
QualifiersBatch (FEFO default), serial, weight are captured by the register's entry chain.This is the only moment these are knowable. Nobody reconstructs which lot left the shop afterwards.
Line existsQuantity, price, discount, and any qualifier ride on the line. No stock has moved yet.An open ticket is not a commitment. Abandoning it must leave no trace in the ledger.
PaidThe tender completes and the ticket becomes immutable.Payment is the commit point — see the invariant below.
Movement postedOne sale movement per line: negative delta, before captured, document = the ticket number, who = the register, cost = unit cost × qty.This is the integration. Everything else is reads.
Derived effectsOn-hand falls; the item may cross its reorder point and appear on the needs-ordering list; stock value and COGS update; a composite depletes components instead of itself.All downstream of the one movement. Nothing recomputes stock from tickets.

Stock moves on payment, not on add-to-ticket. The alternative — decrementing when a line is added — means an abandoned ticket permanently loses stock, and in a queue where tickets are held, cleared and resumed constantly, that drift is unrecoverable. The cost of this choice is a short window where two tills can both sell the last unit; §8 is how that is handled.

5 · What the register reads

Continuously, and cheaply. None of it is authoritative in the register.

ReadUsed for
Sellable productsThe grid. Filtered by Show in POS and by what this location stocks.
CategoriesThe category rail. Owned by the catalogue, not per-register.
Price & unitThe line price and whether the item is sold each, by weight or by length.
On hand at this locationTile badges, the Out state, and the mid-shift stock tab.
Reorder pointWhether "low" is true for this item at this location.
Batches & expiryThe batch picker, FEFO-ordered, with short-dated lots surfaced first.
Unit costStamped onto the sale movement. Never shown to a cashier.
Recipe / componentsKnowing that selling this depletes something else.

6 · What the register writes

Deliberately almost nothing. A till is a narrow pen into the ledger.

1A sale movement per sold line, on payment. Negative, at this location, documented by the ticket number.
2A reversing movement on a refund or a post-payment void — never an edit or a deletion of the original.
3A count correction, from the register's own Stock tab, when a cashier fixes an obviously wrong number mid-shift. It posts as a count movement with a reason, exactly like the module's own count — a till gets no privileged silent path.
4A new item, when a shop rings something not in the catalogue yet. Created with an opening balance posted as a movement, so its history starts honest rather than with a mystery number.
5A quick receive (Full tier only), so a delivery arriving mid-shift can be taken onto stock without leaving the lane. Posts as a normal receipt and re-averages cost.

Everything else — purchase orders, transfers, valuation, supplier terms — is the module's, and the register hands off to it with a single Open inventory action rather than reimplementing any of it.

7 · Returns, voids and the shape of a reversal

The most common place a POS quietly corrupts its own stock.

Wrong
// refund: put it back item.stock[loc] += 2; // or: delete the sale movement
Right
// a new movement, forward in time post(item, loc, +2, { kind: 'sale', qty: +2, doc: 'SO-1047-R', ref: 'Refund of SO-1047', reason: 'customer return' })

A reversal is a new movement in the opposite direction, referencing the original document. The original row is never touched. Two consequences worth stating: the ledger shows that a thing was sold and then came back — which is a fact a buyer needs — and the returned unit's condition becomes a decision rather than an assumption.

CaseStock effect
Return, resellablePositive movement back to the selling location. Available again immediately.
Return, damagedPositive movement, then an immediate adjust out with reason damaged. Two rows, because two things happened — the customer's refund and the business's loss are different events.
ExchangeOne document, two movements: the old item in, the new item out. Never a refund and an unrelated sale.
Void before paymentNothing. No stock moved on add, so nothing reverses. This is the reward for committing on payment.
Void after paymentA reversal, role-gated and reasoned, identical in shape to a refund.
Serial returnedThe serial comes back on hand and its warranty is voided — the serial is the link that makes this possible at all.

8 · Offline, and the oversell window

The register must keep selling with no network. That has an honest cost, and the design pays it deliberately.

Because on-hand is derived from movements, an offline till can keep selling against its last known on-hand and queue its sale movements. On reconnect they replay in timestamp order and the server recomputes. Two tills that both sold the last unit produce a negative on-hand — and that is the correct outcome, not a bug to hide.

1A negative on-hand is surfaced, never clamped to zero. Clamping destroys the evidence that the shop sold something it did not have, which is exactly the thing the owner needs to see.
2The register warns at zero but does not block the sale. A customer standing at the counter with the physical item in their hand is more authoritative than a stock number — refusing the sale to protect the number is the wrong trade.
3A negative resolves the way every other discrepancy does: a count. It appears on the overview as needing attention, and counting the shelf posts the correction with a reason.
4Movements are idempotent on replay, keyed by the ticket number that produced them. A till that syncs twice must not sell the same two bottles twice — this is the same reasoning as the payment idempotency key.

9 · Off · Lite · Full

One data model, three amounts of screen. The merchant never meets a screen their business does not run.

Tier 0
Off
A priced product list. No quantities held, nothing ever out of stock, no Stock tab in the rail.
  • Market stall, bar, food truck
  • Reorders by looking at the shelf
  • Sales post no movements
Tier 1 · default
Lite
One screen: item, price, a quantity you can type over, and Show in POS. Sales decrement. Shipped as Products v2.
  • Corner shop, single salon
  • No orders, counts or transfers
  • Movement log still kept
Tier 2
Full
The module: purchasing, receiving, counts, transfers, composites, valuation, suppliers, batch, serials.
  • Multi-store, warehouse, pharmacy
  • Every capability individually optional
  • Same ledger, more documents

The tier is chosen in one place — Stock control: Off · Lite · Full, sitting with the other register settings beside item entry and tile style. A capability that is off removes its rail item, its fields on the item pane and its column in reports. Turning it on adds them back with the data already there.

10 · Products v2 — the shop that doesn't want an inventory

Your recollection is right, and the reason it exists is worth being precise about.

Products v2 is not a cut-down inventory. It is the Lite tier's whole user interface. Same items, same movements, same ledger underneath — but one screen instead of nine, and no document ever shown to the user. A corner shop gets stock control without ever meeting a purchase order.

A shopkeeper wants to…In Products v2 (Lite)In the module (Full)
Add a productOne row: name, price, quantity, Show in POSItem pane with cost, supplier, reorder, par, batch and serial policy
Fix a wrong quantityType over the number. A movement is posted behind it with reason recountOpen or schedule a count; variance posts in units and money
Take in a deliveryType the new higher numberReceive against a purchase order; cost re-averages; supplier reliability updates
Know what to buyA low list, by reorder pointReorder suggestions with lead time, par level and supplier grouping
Hide a lineShow in POS offSame toggle, same flag
Know what it's worth— not shownValuation, COGS, margin, turnover, shrinkage by reason

The design tension, stated plainly. "Type over the quantity" is a deliberate violation of the stock is never edited rule at the level of the interface — and the resolution is that it only looks like an edit. It still posts a movement with a reason. The shopkeeper gets the affordance they expect; the ledger gets the row it needs; and if that business later turns on Full, its history is already coherent.

11 · Growing a tier, without a migration

Off · a price list→ Lite · quantities appear→ + locations→ + purchasing→ + counts→ Full

Each arrow is a switch, not a project. Turning on locations takes the single implicit location and names it; existing movements already carry it. Turning on purchasing means deliveries stop being "type a bigger number" and start being receipts against an order — and the items, costs and suppliers are already there. Nothing is lost switching back down: the capability's screens disappear, its data stays, and the ledger keeps every row.

This is the commercial argument as much as the technical one. A shop that outgrows Koomzo's Lite tier does not shop around for an inventory system — it turns one on.

12 · When a sale isn't one unit out

Composites & recipes

Selling a gift basket or a cocktail depletes the components, not the parent — several movements under one ticket. The parent may hold no stock at all.

The salon back bar

Colour, developer and foil are ordinary stocked items with Show in POS off. A colour service lists them as consumed on completion; finishing the appointment posts one adjustment per component with reason back-bar use. That is how a salon finally sees true service margin.

Services

Sell time, not units. No stock movement for the service itself — but it may deplete components, book a resource and post a commission.

Weighed goods

Quantity is the weight and price is per kilo. The movement carries the fractional quantity — the one place a non-integer quantity is correct, while the money stays whole francs.

13 · Invariants

1On-hand is derived from movements. No screen sets it directly; every change is a movement with a before, a document and an operator.
2Stock moves on payment, never on add-to-ticket. An abandoned ticket leaves no trace in the ledger.
3A paid ticket and its movements are one transaction. A sale that took money without moving stock, or moved stock without taking money, is a defect — not a state the system may rest in.
4Every sale movement carries the ticket number, so any stock question is answerable back to a receipt, and any receipt forward to its stock effect.
5A correction is a new movement. Nothing in the ledger is ever edited or deleted, by any role.
6On-hand is always per location. A till answers for its own location only; a company total is a report.
7A negative on-hand is legal and visible. It means the shop sold what it did not have, and the fix is a count, not a clamp.
8Movements are idempotent on replay, keyed by their document. Syncing twice must not sell the same unit twice.
9The unit cost stamped on a sale movement is the cost at that moment, so a later delivery re-averaging cost never rewrites past margin.
10Show in POS is the only thing that decides register visibility. Deleting an item to hide it is never the answer — history would go with it.

14 · The gap between this design and the code · closed

Found while writing this, and now fixed. Everything in this section describes the state before that work — see §15 for what shipped.

As found — the register did not post stock movements, and nothing did. iv-store.js has a posting function for every other path — receive, adjust, count, transfer, back-bar — and no postSale. The kind:'sale' rows in the movement log are seed data written by hand. Meanwhile Retail Flex tracks availability in its own local RX_STOCK_SEED map and decrements that, so the register's stock and the module's stock are two unconnected numbers that happen to look consistent in a demo.

What existsState
The ledger and its invariantBuilt — and genuinely good — one post() captures before for every path
Receive, adjust, count, transferBuilt · all posting correctly
Back-bar consumptionBuilt — the salon case works end to end
Register reads availabilityBuilt — but against a local seed map, not the module
Register posts a sale movementMissing entirely
Refund reversalMissing — follows from the above
Composite depletion on saleNever triggered — modelled in data only

What closing it needs

1IVS.postSale(ticket) in iv-store.js — one document, one movement per line, doc = the ticket number, who = the register, cost stamped per line. It is the smallest of the existing posting functions and the most important.
2Composite expansion inside it, so selling a basket posts its components. The recipe is already on the item.
3IVS.reverseSale(ticketNo, lines, condition) for refunds, writing forward-in-time reversals and the damaged-goods adjustment where needed.
4Flex reads the module, not a seed. Replace RX_STOCK_SEED with reads against IV.onHand(item, loc) so there is one on-hand in the product, not two. This is the change that makes the other three mean anything.
5Tier gating at the post, not the call site: Off posts nothing, Lite and Full post identically. One check in one place, so no register can forget it.

Built: Closing the stock-posting gap — eight phases, all shipped (§15). Two findings widened the job and are both resolved: the ledger was module-private, so it moved to the platform as kz/kz-stock.js; and Products v2 loaded no posting engine at all, which made the Lite tier's "type over the quantity" promise unimplementable until it gained one.

Worth noting the ordering risk: invariant 3 says the ticket and its movements are one transaction. Building the sale posting after the payment flow already shipped means that transaction boundary has to be added to code that has no reason to expect it — which was the argument for doing this before more registers started taking money.

15 · What shipped

All eight phases of the implementation plan are in. The seam described in §1–13 is now real code.

The ledger is platform, in two files. kz/kz-stock.js is the universal core — items, locations, movements, post(), on-hand, cost averaging — with no knowledge of tickets or tenders, so a tenant who runs Inventory alone loads it and nothing else. kz/kz-stock-sales.js is the POS's adapter over it, loaded only by registers.

PhaseWhat landed
1 · Platformiv-store.js's ledger moved to kz-stock.js; the module binds IV_ITEMS/IV_MOVES to it so its screens read the very same objects the registers post into. post() and reaverage() in the module are now one line each.
2 · postSaleOne document, one movement per line, through the core's post(). Tier read from the tenant, never passed in. Returns the negatives it created so the caller can surface them without the ledger knowing about UI.
3 · FlexRX_STOCK_SEED is gone. Tiles, the Stock tab and the low list read KZ_STOCK.onHand(); the stepper posts a count correction with a reason. Verified end to end: selling one Ibuprofène took on-hand 7 → 6 and wrote sale −1, before 7, doc DLA1-C1-0918-1047, lot B24-0871, who Caisse 1, cost 504 — the batch captured by the qualifier chain riding onto the movement.
4 · ReversalsreverseSale writes forward-in-time positives referencing the original; the sale row is never touched. Flex's returns screen gained a Condition of the goods control — damaged posts the unit back on hand and straight out again to shrinkage, because the customer's refund and the business's loss are two facts.
5 · Products v2The Lite tier had no posting engine at all — and, it turned out, was not rendering either: it was missing iv-sheetkit.jsx, so useIVRev was undefined and the page threw on mount. Both fixed; 18 items now render and the tier's promise is implementable.
6 · Other registersGrocery (20 items), Salon (7 retail lines) and Gym (10 bar/shop lines) each adopt their catalogue into the one ledger and post at their own commit point. Salon posts products only — a service's back-bar consumption is posted when the appointment completes, and double-posting it would halve true service margin.
7 · Compositesexpand() inside postSale: an item with a recipe posts movements for its components, and the row carries via so the parent is traceable.
8 · IdempotencyKeyed on the ticket number. Verified: posting the same ticket twice moves on-hand once and returns replayed: true.

Three decisions taken while building

1The commit point moved. Sale posting was first wired to the charge sheet's done callback — which fires when the cashier taps New order, not when the money lands. A ticket paid and then abandoned would have taken money and moved no stock. useCharge gained an onPaid hook that fires the moment the balance reaches zero, which is the only honest reading of invariant 3.
2The tier is a tenant capability now, not a Retail Flex tweak — an Inventory-only business has no register to hold it, and Off is incoherent for a tenant whose whole module is stock. The Flex control still exists as a preview shortcut, but it writes KZ_STOCK.tier.
3A preset switch seeds real items. Flex's nine demo trades are different shops, so each seeds its products into the ledger once with a real opening-balance movement, rather than the ledger holding nine parallel item sets. Non-Flex modules adopt their catalogue under a namespaced id (grocery:p1) so a shop's plantain and a salon's shampoo cannot collide.

Left deliberately undone. Restaurant does not post: its stock event is fire, not payment, because a cooked dish has consumed its ingredients whether the table pays or not. Folding it in would have corrupted the post-on-payment rule. It needs its own short spec on recipe depletion at fire time.
