Inventory — information architecture & POS integration

How stock is modelled once and consumed everywhere: the register, the salon, the kiosk. Answers the four questions in the brief — catalogue population, service authoring, how small a small shop can go, and where the module lives inside Retail and Retail Flex.

Inventory module Products v2 (Lite) Retail Flex · Stock tab

1 · One object, four faces

There is no separate "product database" and "POS catalogue". There is one Item, and the register is one of its channels.

Item

Name, SKU, barcode, price, cost, category, type. Everything a merchant types once. Four types: product, service, composite, non-stock.

Stock

A quantity per item × location. Only products carry it. Services and gift cards have none, and the UI says so rather than showing a zero.

Movement

Every change of quantity is a row: sale, receipt, transfer, adjust, count. Nothing edits a quantity silently — that single rule is what makes shrinkage readable.

Channel

The pos block on the item: show/hide, register category, tile style. This is the join between inventory and the register — nothing else crosses.

2 · How products reach the POS catalogue

Explicit, per item, with a live preview of the tile the cashier will see. No implicit sync, no second catalogue to maintain.

1
Author the item once, in Inventory
Items → New item. Name, SKU, barcode, cost, price. The barcode is what a scan resolves to at the lane.
2
Turn on Show in POS
On the item's POS tab. Off is a real state, not an oversight: back-bar colour, developer and foil are stocked, ordered and counted, but must never appear on a register grid.
3
Pick a register category and tile style
Category drives the chip rail on the board; tile style picks image tile, text key or list row. The pane renders the exact tile as you choose — the merchant never guesses.
4
The register reads, never writes
The board renders every item with pos.show = true for its location. Availability comes from the same stock record: low shows a count on the tile, out dims the tile.
5
Selling posts a movement back
One sale row against the selling location. A composite depletes its recipe instead of itself. A service depletes only what it consumes.
Why a toggle rather than automatic. Auto-publish looks tidy until a salon has 40 back-bar SKUs, a warehouse holds packaging, and a kiosk should sell 12 lines out of 300. Bulk-select on the Items list makes the toggle cheap for the common case: select all, Show in POS, done.

3 · Services — same catalogue, different physics

A salon service is an Item with type = service. It sits in the same list, uses the same pricing and tax, and appears on the register through the same POS toggle. What changes is what it holds.

FieldProductService
QuantityOn hand per locationNone. Capacity is staff time, not units
Duration—Service time + clean-up buffer, feeds Appointments
Who—Staff who may perform it; optional price per stylist level
Resource—Chair, colour bar, treatment room
CostUnit cost, re-averaged on receiptBack-bar consumption × component cost
On saleOne unit outBooks time, posts commission, depletes consumption list
The back bar is the whole trick. Colour, developer and foil are ordinary stocked products with Show in POS off. A colour service lists them under Consumes on completion with a quantity. Completing the appointment posts one adjustment per component with reason Back-bar use — so the salon finally sees its true service margin, and reorders colour on data instead of memory.

Services that should never be sold at the register — a patch test, a consultation — simply keep Show in POS off while staying bookable online. Same toggle, same mental model.

4 · Does every retail POS need an inventory module?

No. Three tiers, one module, one data model. The merchant moves up a tier without a migration, and never sees a screen their business does not run.

Tier 0
Off
A fixed product list with prices. No quantities, nothing ever out of stock.
  • Market stall, bar, food truck
  • Rail shows no Stock tab
Tier 1 · default
Lite
One screen: item, price, a quantity you can type over, and Show in POS. Sales decrement it.
  • Corner shop, single salon
  • No orders, counts or transfers
  • Shipped as Products v2
Tier 2
Full
The module: purchasing, receiving, counts, transfers, composites, valuation, suppliers.
  • Multi-store, warehouse, pharmacy
  • Every capability individually optional

Progressive disclosure is enforced in one place — the capability map in Setup. A capability that is off removes its rail item, its fields on the item pane and its column in reports. Turning it on adds them back with the data already there; nothing is migrated, nothing is lost switching down.

5 · Where it lives inside the POS

Same shell, same rail, same gestures. Inventory is not a different application the cashier has to learn.

In the register — a Stock rail item

Second position, right after Register. What a lane actually needs mid-shift: on-hand for this location, a stepper to correct a count, Show in POS, and a needs-ordering list. In Full mode it adds quick receive so a delivery can be scanned in without leaving the lane.

An Open inventory button hands off to the full module for anything deeper.

On the board — availability, not surprise

With stock control on, tiles carry the truth: a count badge when low, a dimmed tile and Out flag at zero. Hidden items disappear from the grid entirely. Nothing else about the register changes.

In Setup — one selector

Stock control: Off · Lite · Full sits with the other register settings, beside item entry and tile style. It is the same three tiers, chosen where every other capability of the board is chosen.

Across modules

Salon Appointments reads services and posts back-bar consumption. The self-serve kiosk reads the same pos.show flag. Retail and Retail Flex share one catalogue; a location decides which subset a device sells.
