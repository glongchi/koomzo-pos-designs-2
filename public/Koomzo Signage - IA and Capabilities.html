Signage — information architecture & capabilities

A screen module for a platform that already knows the prices, the menu, the stock and the queue. Signage owns the players, the playlists and the layouts; it owns none of the content. Designed for a 4G dongle that drops for hours at a time, a TV stick with a small cache, and a manager who authors on a desktop and checks from a phone.

The one design decision

Every standalone signage product on the market asks the operator to retype their menu. This one refuses to store a single price.

1
A player is a Device, not a new registry
A TV stick, a kiosk browser, a smart TV, a spare tablet or the till’s second screen all pair with a six-digit code and become a Device with a signage profile. The shell already owns devices, sites and pairing — signage adds orientation, cache state and a heartbeat.
2
A board holds a query, never prices
A menu board is bound to a category; a price screen to an item set; a rate card to hotel rate plans. The server resolves it at play time. Change a price at the till and the wall agrees within one heartbeat — with no republish, no re-authoring, no second catalogue to keep in step.
3
A playlist is an ordered list of references with durations
Media, board, notice, ticker, now-serving, wayfinding. Each item can carry a condition — a daypart, a day mask, or a data condition such as stock > 0. Publishing bumps a version; players finish the item they are on and switch cleanly.
4
The player caches whole versions and plays offline for days
A loop version becomes playable only when every referenced asset is present and checksum-verified. On a dead line the player keeps playing the last complete version and batches its proof-of-play for later. A screen never blanks — that is the module’s first invariant.
Signage holds no transaction and no content of its own. It is a passive renderer: it plays media, resolves boards and follows a schedule. Self-service ordering and check-in stay in the Restaurant and Salon kiosks, which own the state machine, the payment gateway and the printer. A greyed-out line is Inventory saying zero. The module’s entire ownership is: players, groups, loops, assets, board definitions, schedule rules, takeovers and play records.

The offline contract

You chose to assume an unreliable line. That is not a fallback path here — it is the normal operating mode, and it decides the API shape.

One read, not many

GET /v1/signage/manifest?player=&since= returns the player’s whole contract in a single ETag’d document: resolved loop version, asset list with checksums, schedule rules, and any active takeover. A player on a bad line makes one request, not twelve.

Whole versions only

A version is playable when every asset checksums. Until then the player stays on the previous complete version. There is no state in which a loop is half-updated in public.

Heartbeat is the only failure signal

POST /heartbeat carries state, current item, cache bytes and app version — and is also how a takeover is picked up. Missing heartbeats past the tenant’s tolerance is what raises attention; nothing else is inferred.

Idempotent play batches

Play records are written on the player and uploaded in batches, idempotent on (player, item, started_at). Uptime gaps are reported as gaps — playback is never backfilled or estimated.

The socket is a nicety, not the contract. A signage estate on 4G cannot depend on a held connection. The player is correct with polling alone; a push only makes an update arrive in seconds instead of within the heartbeat interval. Any design where a missed socket frame means stale content on a wall is the wrong design for this market.

Open questions

Five of the seven are now settled. Each resolution is recorded with the reason, because the reasons are what a later maintainer will need.

Board rendering — resolved: payload, with a declared fallback. Hardware-agnostic is the whole strategy, so the player declares its capabilities at pairing and a panel that cannot do web fonts or CSS Grid asks for a server-rendered image of the same board. One board definition, two render paths, chosen by the device rather than by the author.
Cache ceiling — resolved: refuse at author time. The player reports cache_bytes in every heartbeat, so publish can compute the weight against the thinnest player in scope and block with a real number. Silently dropping a video means the operator’s campaign did not run and nobody was told.
Till second screen — resolved: Retail keeps it, signage fills the idle gap. A customer-facing display needs sub-second reactivity for ring-ups, tax and tender; routing that through a manifest poll would be latency for no gain. The POS drives the screen during a sale and hands rendering to an embedded player after N idle seconds, reclaiming it the instant an item is scanned.
Touch screens — resolved: dropped. Self-service ordering and check-in need state machines, a payment gateway and a printer. A general-purpose interaction builder inside a playback engine is a fragile abstraction with two owners; Restaurant and Salon kiosks keep their own. Signage stays a passive renderer, and the freed slot became interactive = idle handover.
Is proof of play a commercial feature or fleet assurance? It only earns its Full-tier place if tenants sell space on their screens. If not, it is uptime reporting and belongs beside the health list.
Multi-site conflict story. Does a branch manager editing a group loop fork it, or is a per-player override the only permitted local write?
Naming — resolved: playlist in the interface, loop in the schema. Every manager who has met a screen already knows the word playlist; the engine is genuinely cyclic and the code should say so. One-to-one mapping, documented, and no user-facing string ever says loop.
Does the idle-handover timeout belong to signage or to the register? It reads as a signage behaviour, but the screen is Retail-owned — so the toggle probably sits in register settings with signage supplying only the playlist.

What comes next

The registry and rail are in place, so the module is already resolvable by Home, Apps and Setup: a Lite tenant sees Screens, Playlists, Media and Boards and nothing else. Next build is the Signage page itself on the shared shell — Screens as the landing view with a live fleet list, then Boards, since the price-board binding is the thing no competitor has. Then the player node: pairing screen, playback engine, and the filesystem cache, which is where the real engineering risk sits.
