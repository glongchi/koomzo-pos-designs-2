Implementation plan · §14 of the integration spec

Making a paid ticket move stock

Delivered — all eight phases are implemented and verified; see §15 of the integration spec for what shipped. Eight changes across three layers, not two. The short answer to your question is yes — both the Inventory and the register modules need work — but the analysis turned up something the integration doc understated: the ledger is currently module-private, and the Lite tier that ships to small shops has no posting engine loaded at all. That changes the shape of the work from "add a function and call it" to "promote the ledger to the platform, then call it from five registers."

1 · Does this need both modules? Yes — and a third thing

LayerWhat changesWhy it cannot be avoided
InventoryGains postSale, reverseSale, composite expansion, idempotency keyingThe ledger and its one post() primitive live here. A sale is a movement kind, and only this module may write movements.

2 · Two discoveries that widen the job

Discovery 1 — the ledger is module-private. iv-store.js is loaded by exactly one page: Koomzo POS - Inventory.html. No register loads any iv-* file. So §14's "Flex reads the module" is not a swap of one function call — the two modules currently cannot reference each other at all. The ledger has to move to the platform layer first.

Discovery 2 — the Lite tier has no posting engine. Koomzo POS - Products v2.html loads iv-data.js but not iv-store.js. So the tier we ship to corner shops — the one whose whole promise is "type over the quantity and a movement is posted behind it" — has no IVS object in scope. That promise is currently unimplementable on that page, which makes it the most important line in this plan, not a footnote.

Both discoveries point the same way: the ledger is infrastructure, not a feature of one screen. The suite already made this move once, for money — kz-tender.js, kz-policy.js, kz-ticket.js. Stock is the same kind of thing and belongs in the same place.

3 · Blast radius — who sells stocked goods

Every surface that takes money for a physical thing needs to post. Five do; §14 named one.

SurfaceSellsWhat it needs
Retail FlexStocked productsThe full change: drop the local map, read the ledger, post on payment, reverse on refund. The reference implementation.
Grocery tillStocked productsSame as Flex, plus its promotions are already applied at tender — the posting hooks the same commit point.
SalonRetail products + servicesProducts post like Flex. Services post nothing — but a service with a back-bar list already posts consumption, so that path exists and must not be duplicated.
GymPro shop & juice barBar and shop sales are ordinary stocked sales. Memberships and PT are not stock at all.
RestaurantMenu items with recipesThe hardest, and the one I would defer — see Your call. A dish depletes ingredients, which is composite expansion at every fire, not at payment.
HotelFolio chargesNothing. A room night is not stock. F&B postings originate in Restaurant and post there.

Restaurant breaks the "post on payment" rule, and that is not a bug in the rule. A fired dish has consumed its ingredients whether or not the table ever pays. So hospitality's stock event is fire, not payment — which is a genuinely different commit point and deserves its own decision rather than being folded into this plan quietly.

5 · Does C cover a tenant who only uses Inventory?

Structurally yes — that is the main argument for it. But not as I specified it in the phases below, and the gap is worth fixing before phase 1 rather than after.

First, the scenario is real and already supported. kz-caps.js carries Inventory as a first-class module — core: 'Items', "The catalogue. Never hideable." A wholesaler, a distributor, a spare-parts dépôt or an NGO stock room is a legitimate Koomzo tenant with no register at all. Any ledger design has to serve it.

Why C is the right direction for them. Options A and B both assume a register exists: A loads the POS's dependencies into the ledger's page, and B makes the register the owner of stock truth with Inventory reconciling to it. Under C, Inventory is simply one consumer of the platform — the same relationship Flex has, with no register in the picture. An Inventory-only tenant loads kz-stock.js and its own UI, full stop.

The proof that the core is genuinely universal is already in the data model: the movement kinds include issue, so a tenant that ships goods out on a delivery note or to a job has a path that is not sale. Stock leaving is not a POS concept. Money changing hands at a till is.

But three things in my phases assume a register — all fixable now

HoleWhat breaks with no registerFix
The tier has no homeOff · Lite · Full is currently a Retail Flex tweak (rx-app.jsx, and the selector in Flex's own Setup screen). No register means no tier — and Off is incoherent for a tenant whose entire module is stock.Move the tier to a tenant capability the register reads rather than owns. Note the Register Spec already calls it "a tenant switch" while the code makes it a register setting — the two already disagree, and this forces the resolution.
postSale is POS vocabularyPhase 2 puts postSale, reverseSale and tier gating in kz-stock.js — so an Inventory-only tenant loads sale posting it can never call, and the ledger takes a dependency on KZ_TICKET for a document type it may never see.Split the file. kz-stock.js = the universal core. kz-stock-sales.js = the POS's entry into it. Inventory-only loads the core alone.
who assumes a tillMy phase-2 contract passes register: 'Caisse 1' and stamps who: register. An Inventory-only tenant's actor is a user in a session — a storekeeper receiving a delivery, not a lane.A neutral actor on every movement: { kind: 'register' | 'user', label }. Otherwise inventory-only rows are blank or lie, and "who moved this" is the ledger's whole point.

The shape of the correction: C stands, but the platform layer is two files, not one — a universal stock core, and a sales adapter over it. That also answers the reverse asymmetry cleanly: Hotel needs neither, Restaurant will need a third adapter for fire-time depletion, and none of them force their vocabulary on the others. Phases 1 and 2 below are written against the single-file version and are corrected accordingly.

One more consequence worth naming. Under the split, Products v2 becomes the Inventory-only tenant's whole product at the Lite tier — a one-screen stock system with no register anywhere. That is a coherent thing to sell, and it is only coherent if the tier is a tenant capability rather than a register tweak.

Phase 1 · The ledger becomes platform

PlatformInventoryno behaviour change

iv-store.js becomes kz/kz-stock.js, exposing window.KZ_STOCK. The existing IVS global stays as an alias so no existing call site changes — this phase must be provably behaviour-neutral, because everything after it depends on the module still working exactly as it does now.

Corrected by §5: the platform layer is two files. kz-stock.js holds only what is universal — items, locations, movements, post(), on-hand, cost averaging, the actor concept — and takes no dependency on KZ_TICKET. Sale posting lands in kz-stock-sales.js in phase 2. An Inventory-only tenant loads the core and nothing else.

Also in this phase: the stock tier moves out of Flex's tweaks into a tenant capability, since the core has to answer "is this tenant tracking quantities" without asking a register.

The item and movement collections move with it. Inventory's screens keep reading IV_ITEMS and IV.onHand(); those become thin re-exports of the platform's.

Done when: the Inventory module behaves identically — receive, adjust, count, transfer, back-bar all still post — and KZ_STOCK is reachable from a page that loads none of the iv-* UI.

Phase 2 · postSale

2The function that closes the gap
Inventorythe core change
kz/kz-stock-sales.js · new

One document, one movement per line, routed through the core's existing post() so before is captured exactly as every other path captures it. This file is the POS's adapter onto the ledger — nothing an Inventory-only tenant loads.

Contract
KZ_SALES.postSale({ ticketNo, // the document locId, // where it sold actor, // { kind:'register', label:'Caisse 1' } lines: [{ id, qty, lot, serial }], }) → { doc, rows, negatives: [] } // tier is READ from the tenant // capability, never passed in
Per line
KZ_STOCK.post(id, locId, -qty, { kind: 'sale', doc: ticketNo, ref: 'Ticket ' + short(ticketNo), partner: customer || 'Walk-in', actor, // user or register cost: qty * item.cost, // at this moment lot, serial })

Tier gating lives here, not at the call site — Off posts nothing and returns cleanly; Lite and Full post identically. One check in one place means no register can forget it, which is the same reasoning that put the tender list in the platform. It reads the tenant capability rather than trusting a caller's argument, so a register cannot claim a tier it does not have.

Returns the negatives it created, so the caller can surface "this sold below zero" without the ledger having to know about UI.

Phase 3 · Flex reads the ledger

3Delete the second truth
Registerthe change that makes the rest mean anything
retailflex/rx-inv.jsxretailflex/rx-app.jsxretailflex/rx-parts.jsx

RX_STOCK_SEED and the stock useState go. Tiles, the Stock tab and the low list all read KZ_STOCK.onHand(item, loc); the Stock tab's stepper posts a count correction instead of mutating a local object. InventoryView's onStock prop disappears entirely.

Then the actual wiring: on payment, postSale. Flex already has everything it needs — the ticket number from KZ_TICKET, the lines, the operator.

A real subtlety. Flex's catalogue is per-preset (nine trades of demo products) while the ledger holds one item set. Either the presets seed real ledger items on switch, or the ledger gains per-preset item sets. I lean to the first — a preset switch is a different shop, so re-seeding is honest — but it is a decision, not a detail.

Done when: selling two units on the register makes the number fall in the Inventory module's own Stock screen, with a sale row carrying the ticket number.

Phase 4 · Reversals

4Refunds, exchanges, post-payment voids
InventoryRegister
kz/kz-stock.jsretailflex/rx-screens.jsxretailflex/rx-ops.jsx

reverseSale({ ticketNo, lines, condition }) writes forward-in-time positives referencing the original document — never an edit, never a delete. condition: 'resellable' | 'damaged' decides whether a second adjust row follows it out with reason damaged.

Flex's refund screen gains the resellable/damaged question. It is one control and it is the difference between a shop knowing its shrinkage and guessing it.

Exchange is one document with both movements — the old item in, the new out — not a refund plus an unrelated sale.

Risks

1Phase 1 touches every Inventory screen. Mitigation: the IVS alias means zero call sites change, and the phase ships only when the module is provably identical. If it is not behaviour-neutral, stop rather than proceed.
2The transaction boundary is being added late. Invariant 3 says the ticket and its movements are one transaction, but the payment flow already shipped without one. postSale has to be called where a failure can still fail the sale — in this prototype that is a sequencing discipline; in production it is a real transaction, and it is the single most important thing for the backend to get right.
3Flex's per-preset catalogue versus one ledger item set. Called out in phase 3 and needs your answer before that phase starts, because both options are defensible and they are hard to swap later.
4Demo data becomes load-bearing. The seeded kind:'sale' rows must be removed when real posting lands, or the ledger will double-count its own history. Small, easy to forget, visibly wrong to an owner reading a report.
5Scope creep toward "just sync it". The tempting shortcut is to keep register-local stock and reconcile periodically. That is option B, it is the current bug, and it should be refused explicitly whenever it resurfaces.

How we'll know it works

The round trip

Sell 2 on the register → the Inventory module's Stock screen shows the number fall and a sale row with that ticket number. One number, two screens.

The reversal

Refund one as damaged → two rows appear: the unit back on hand, then straight out again with reason damaged. The original row is untouched.

The replay

Call postSale twice for one ticket → on-hand moves once. This is the offline case, tested without needing to go offline.

The tiers

Off sells and posts nothing. Lite and Full produce byte-identical movements — the tier changes screens, never the ledger.

The basket

Sell one composite → components fall, the parent does not.

The oversell

Sell the last 2 twice → on-hand goes negative, is visible, and is not clamped to zero.

The calls, and how they went

All five were taken on my own recommendation, for a market where a shop's stock record is often its only accounting.

1
Decided: out. Not wired. Its stock event is fire, not payment — a cooked dish has consumed its ingredients whether the table pays or not — so folding it in would either corrupt the "post on payment" rule or special-case it invisibly. It deserves its own short spec on recipe depletion at fire time.
2
Decided: presets seed real ledger items, once each, with a real opening-balance movement. Non-Flex modules adopt under a namespaced id so a grocery's plantain and a salon's shampoo cannot collide.
3
How far do I take phase 1? Minimal is iv-store.js → kz-stock.js with an alias. Fuller is also moving IV_ITEMS/IV_MOVES and making Inventory a pure UI layer. Decided: fuller — the core owns items, movements and posting; the module binds its arrays to it and keeps the documents. The core/adapter split shipped in this phase, as §5 required.
4
Decided: warn, never block. A negative posts, is surfaced in a toast with a Count shortcut, and is never clamped. In this market refusing a sale to protect a number loses a real customer standing at the counter — and the number is the thing more likely to be wrong. A per-trade hard stop stays open for pharmacy and serialised goods.
5
Decided: moved. KZ_STOCK.tier is the tenant's; Flex's control writes it rather than owning it. It is a Flex tweak today, which leaves an Inventory-only tenant with no tier at all and makes Off incoherent for them. Moving it is a small change with one visible consequence: the Stock control selector leaves the register's Setup screen and joins the capability map, so an owner sets it once for the business rather than per register.
