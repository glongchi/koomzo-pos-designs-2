Functional specification · high level

The Register, across Koomzo modules

Two register surfaces ship in Koomzo today — Retail Flex and Restaurant & Bar. They are not two products: they are one transaction model wearing two shells, each shaped by how its trade identifies a ticket, how it fulfils it, and how it gets paid. Flex renders itself in four device compositions, the phone one of them. This document states the shared model, the business process around it, and what each shell adds or removes. §6 and §9 are kept as the record of the two registers that merged in — see §20.

1 · Scope

What this document covers, and the one sentence every register answers to.

The register's job: turn a customer's intent into a priced, taxed, paid and recorded transaction — in the fewest taps the trade allows, on the device the operator already holds, whether or not the network is up.

In scope

Item entry, ticket construction, pricing and tax, holds and splits, tender and settlement, fulfilment handoff, session and drawer close, and the differences between the four module shells.

Out of scope

Catalogue authoring, stock replenishment, procurement, payroll, accounting postings, and the Kitchen Display / Floor Plan / Kiosk screens — referenced here only where the register hands off to them.

2 · Business context

Every Koomzo tenant is Cameroonian or West African. That is a functional constraint, not a localisation detail.

ConstraintConsequence for the register
Currency XAFNo minor unit. Every amount is a whole franc, grouped with a space — 12 500 FCFA long form, 12 500 F in dense tables and tiles. A decimal point in a price is a defect. Cash rounding is meaningful: the smallest coin in circulation is 5 F.
TVA 19,25 %One national, single-rated tax. There is no per-city sales tax and no tax-group picker at the till. Zero-rated trades (fresh food, some pharmacy lines) are a profile property, not a per-line choice by the cashier.
Mobile money firstThe tender list is ordered MTN MoMo · Orange Money · Espèces · Carte bancaire. Card is the fourth option, not the default. Customer-account credit and (in hospitality) room charge extend the list.
Unreliable connectivityThe register must ring, price, hold and tender offline, then reconcile. Sequence numbers are issued locally; the server is authoritative on conflict.
Shared, mixed devicesOne phone can be a full register; the same tenant may run a tablet on the floor and a laptop at the counter. Layout adapts; the transaction model does not.
Operator literacy & turnoverCopy is terse, imperative and label-driven; destructive actions are colour-coded coral and require a role. A new cashier must be productive in one shift.

3 · Core concepts

The vocabulary the four shells share. Where a trade uses a different word for the same object, both are listed.

ConceptDefinition & business meaning
Register / TillA named selling point (Caisse 1, Lane 1) bound to a location, a cash drawer and a device. Sales, sequence numbers and the drawer count all belong to it.
Session / ShiftOne cashier's period on one register: opening float → sales → cash drops → count → close. Nothing is sold outside an open session.
Ticket / Order / BillThe unit of sale. Retail calls it a ticket, hospitality an order or a bill. Carries a number, an owner (operator), an optional customer, an optional identity (table, tab, room) and its lines.
LineOne product at one qualified configuration: quantity, unit price (possibly overridden), line discount, and qualifiers — variant, batch, serial, weight, modifiers, notes, course, station.
Qualifier stepA question the register must answer before an item can join the ticket: age check, prescription sign-off, variant, batch/expiry, serial, weight, add-ons. Which steps exist is a capability, not code.
Hold / ParkAn open ticket set aside so the operator can serve someone else. Retail parks to a hold list; hospitality parks implicitly against a table or a named tab.
Fire / SendHospitality only: committing part of a ticket to production. Fired lines leave the cashier's control and appear on the kitchen or bar display.
CourseA firing group within one ticket (1st, 2nd, 3rd…) so a kitchen paces a table rather than cooking everything at once.
Cover / GuestHeadcount on a table. Drives per-guest splits, service charge and the average-cover figures management reads.
TenderOne payment instrument applied to a ticket. A ticket may need several tenders; it is paid only when the tendered sum reaches the total.
SplitDividing one ticket — by guest, by line, or by amount — into several settlements. It does not create several tickets in the sales history.
Return / ExchangeA ticket derived from an earlier receipt: selected lines come back, money or replacement goods go out, stock is restocked.
ReceiptThe customer-facing record: printed, emailed, or shown. It is also the key that makes a return possible.
X / Z reportX = mid-shift read, no reset. Z = close: takings by tender, expected cash, counted cash, over/short.

4 · Ticket lifecycle

One state machine covers all four modules. Hospitality inserts the fired transition; retail skips it.

10 · Item entry chain

Between "tap a product" and "a line exists" sits a fixed-order chain of optional questions. Order is invariant; the active subset is configuration.

Tap / scan→ Age→Rx→ Variant→Batch→ Serial→Weight→ Add-ons→ Line on ticket
StepQuestionRule
AgeIs the customer old enough?Fires only on restricted products. Two outcomes: ID verified (recorded on the receipt) or refuse sale (line never created).
RxWho prescribed this, and was advice given?Pharmacy only. Sign-off is stored against the line for audit.
VariantWhich size, colour, strength, pack?Skipped when a scan already resolved the exact variant. Picker style is a tenant choice: single list, size × colour matrix, or a configurator.
BatchWhich lot, and does it expire first?FEFO default. Expiry travels to the receipt and the stock ledger.
SerialWhich unit is leaving the shop?Warranty starts at sale and is registered against the serial.
WeightHow much is on the scale?Live read or keyed, with tare. Quantity is the weight; price is per kilo.
Add-onsCut, wrap, extras?Modifiers may add to the line price; they print on the ticket and on production tickets.

11 · Pricing, discount & tax

1Line net = unit price (override if set) × quantity × (1 − line discount %). The override is recorded separately from the catalogue price so margin reporting stays honest.
2Discount is entered as a percentage on a line, or as an amount at ticket level. Both are capped at 100 %; a negative total is impossible.
3Service charge (hospitality, table and tab orders only) is applied to the ticket net before tax, at a tenant-set rate.
4TVA is computed on net + service charge at the national rate, and rounded to the whole franc. Zero-rated profiles compute zero — the cashier never picks a tax rate.
5Total = net + service + tax, always a whole franc. Cash change is computed against notes and coins that actually circulate.
6Price overrides and above-threshold discounts are privileged actions: they are logged with the operator, the reason where captured, and appear in the shift report.

12 · Tender & settlement

Choosing how the customer pays is one sheet, ordered the way a Cameroonian counter actually takes money.

TenderBehaviour
MTN MoMo · Orange MoneyDefault pair. Operator confirms the customer's transfer against the displayed amount; reference is stored on the ticket.
Espèces (cash)Quick-cash keys for the total and the next round note; change due is shown large. Drawer opens; the amount counts toward the expected cash at close.
Carte bancaireWaits for the terminal; approval and last four digits land on the receipt.
Loyalty / customer accountApplies a balance or bills a house account. Requires an attached customer.
Charge to roomExists only when the capability is on and a hotel is publishing occupied rooms. Posts to the guest folio and settles at checkout — the register takes no money.
SplitTake a first amount on one tender, the balance on another. Hospitality splits by guest first, then tenders each share.

After payment: print, email, or skip the receipt; the ticket closes, stock and loyalty commit, the table releases, the ticket number increments, and the register returns to an empty ticket. Failure at any point leaves the ticket in Tendering with captured tenders intact — never in a half-paid limbo.

13 · Fulfilment routing

Only hospitality fulfils; the model is worth stating because grocery counters and salons will need the same routing later.

Station, not screen

Every menu item declares a station — grill, fryer, cold, braise, sauce pot, dessert, bar, counter. Firing routes lines to the display that owns that station, colour-coded so a cook reads their own work only.

Course, not batch

Lines are grouped into courses. Firing a course sends its unfired lines and marks them fired; later courses wait for the server's call, so a table is paced rather than flooded.

Bar is immediate

Bar lines are not held for coursing — drinks go as soon as they are rung, which is why bar lines are excluded from the kitchen send.

Notes carry weight

Allergen tags and line notes travel with the production ticket. An allergen tag is a safety instruction, not a preference, and is never truncated on the kitchen display.

15 · Invariants

Server-enforced. The client mirrors them for speed; the server decides.

1The register is the core of its module and can never be switched off. Every other screen in the module is a capability that can be hidden.
2A plain line merges with an identical plain line; a line carrying a variant, batch, serial, weight or modifier never merges — it is a distinct unit of sale.
3Setting a line's quantity to zero removes the line. There is no zero-quantity line on a ticket.
4A barcode scan that resolves to an exact variant skips the variant question. Asking again is a defect.
5A fired line cannot be silently edited. Changing it emits a void-and-refire the production display sees.
6A hospitality order cannot be fired without a table or a named tab — the kitchen must know where the food goes.
7A paid ticket is immutable. Corrections are refunds or exchanges against the receipt, never edits.
8Amounts are whole francs everywhere — display, storage and print. Fractional currency is invalid input.
9Ticket numbers are unique per register per session, issued locally so that an offline till keeps selling, and reconciled on sync.
10Every subtractive action — void, refund, price override, above-threshold discount — is attributed to an operator and appears in the session report.
11Touch targets on any register surface are at least 44 px. The register is used with thumbs, in a hurry.

16 · Capability model

How the same register serves a one-person kiosk and a multi-lane store without becoming two products.

Core is fixed

Each module declares one core screen that is never hideable — Register for Retail, Order entry for Restaurant, Till for Grocery. It is the reason the module exists.

Everything else is a capability

Sales history, stock, returns, customers, categories, shift, floor plan, course firing: each is switchable per tenant, and the register reads the switches rather than knowing about them.

Fits are shortcuts

Solo shop, Lite and Full are named bundles of those switches, so an owner picks a shape in one tap and adjusts later. A deviation is shown as "modified", not hidden.

Growth is a switch

Adding stock control, a second lane or course firing never means migrating to another product — it means turning something on. The ticket model is unchanged.

19 · One register, four compositions

The resolved architecture. §6 and §9 describe two registers that no longer exist as code; this states what replaced them and the rule that follows.

There is one retail register. Retail's six fixed profiles became presets in Flex's editable capability map. The Mobile register's three phone patterns became layout options in the same board. What used to be three files is one register that renders itself four ways.

CompositionWhere the ticket livesWhy
Laptop / counterDocked pane, cart rightBoth hands free and a keyboard present. Digits and backspace drive the selected line; a scanner is a keyboard.
Tablet landscapeDocked pane, tightenedSame board, larger targets, narrower cart. The totals footer and charge bar stay pinned.
Tablet portraitDrawer over a scrimNot enough width for two panes. The drawer is dismissible and nothing is lost by closing it.
PhoneBar, sheet or tab — a tenant choiceThere is no single right answer at 390 px, and the operator has a preference. Bar keeps the total visible; sheet keeps the reach shortest; tab gives the ticket the whole screen.

The rule this creates

1A composition changes where the ticket is and nothing else. Same presets, same capability map, same qualifier chain, same tender model, same approval step, same ticket number. A phone is never a reduced register.
2This matters more here than it would elsewhere: in this market a phone is frequently the only till in the business. A phone that cannot age-check a sale, take a pharmacy sign-off, choose a batch or weigh produce is not a lightweight register — it is a shop that cannot legally trade.
3The side drawer is the tablet-portrait answer and is not offered on a phone, where it is simply a worse sheet.
4No register feature may depend on a hover state, a second monitor, or a permanently visible cart — because in three of the four compositions the cart is not permanently visible. Anything that cannot be reached from a bottom sheet belongs on a sibling screen.
5Hospitality keeps its own shell. Order entry leads with the order, not the catalogue, because a server's question is "what is on table 7" — that is a different composition problem, not a fifth option in this list.

20 · Mobile money mechanics

In West and Central Africa mobile money is the primary rail, so the register must model it as a real payment state machine — not as a cash-like instant tender. This section states the mechanics the tender sheet is built on; it is what makes decision 5 below answerable.

The shape of the rail. MTN MoMo's Collections product works by push request: Koomzo posts a requestToPay carrying the amount, currency, an externalId of our own and the payer's MSISDN; the payer is then asked to authorise the payment on their own handset, and the transaction sits in PENDING until they do. Koomzo learns the outcome two ways — a provider callback, or polling the transaction by its reference until it succeeds or fails. Orange Money and Airtel Money differ in wire format but not in shape; Airtel, for instance, reports pending as TIP/TI and success as TS.

ConsequenceRule for the register
Three tender modes, not onePush (register requests, customer approves on their phone — the target experience), pull/attest (customer pays a merchant code themselves, operator confirms), and manual (no integration; operator records the tender). A tenant may have only one of these live; the sheet shows what is provisioned.
Pending is a first-class stateA push tender enters Pending, not Paid. The ticket stays in Tendering with a visible countdown and the amount locked. The operator may cancel and switch tender; they may not mark it paid by hand while a push is live.
The reference is ours, not theirsKoomzo generates the idempotency key and externalId before the request leaves the till. The provider's own transaction id is stored alongside when it arrives. This is why decision 5 goes the way it does.
Timeouts are normalCustomers walk out of coverage, run out of balance, or ignore the prompt. Treat no answer inside the window as expired — a clean, non-scary outcome the operator can retry or replace, never an error dialog.
Never double-charge on retryRetry reuses the same idempotency key; a new attempt gets a new key and is a new tender line. An unknown outcome is resolved by status query, never by re-requesting.
Refunds are a different productMoney going back out is a disbursement (transfer/deposit), with its own float, its own permissions and its own failure modes. A cash refund against a MoMo sale must be an explicit, role-gated tenant policy.
Offline push is impossibleA push tender requires the network by definition. Offline, only attest and manual modes are offered, and each is queued for reconciliation — this is the single place where offline capability is genuinely reduced.
Per-operator float mattersMoMo takings do not sit in the drawer. The Z report reconciles cash and each wallet separately; "expected cash" excludes wallet takings entirely.
Request sent→ Pending · customer approves→ Succeeded /Declined /Expired /Unknown → query

21 · Decisions & rationale

Recommendations for the seven open questions, decided for a West African market: thin margins, high staff turnover, intermittent networks, wallet-first money, and owners who read one number a day.

21.1 · Converge Retail and Retail Flex — yes

Decision: ship one retail register. Flex's editable capability map becomes the model; Retail's six fixed profiles become first-run presets inside it. Retail's file stays as a reference until the merge lands, then retires.

Why: two retail registers means two of everything — two QA surfaces, two training paths, two sets of bugs — for a difference the shopkeeper never asked for. More importantly, a fixed profile is a growth trap: the shop that adds a pharmacy counter or starts weighing rice must change products rather than flip a switch. Presets get the one-tap setup Retail was protecting, without the lock-in. Guardrail: the capability editor is owner/manager-only, and deviations show as "modified" so support can always ask "which preset, and what changed?"

21.2 · Partial payment across a restart — persist it

Decision: tenders are persisted the moment each one is captured. A restart mid-split resumes the ticket in Tendering with captured tenders intact and the balance shown.

Why: the market makes this non-negotiable. Phones die, batteries are shared, power cuts are routine, and a split bill takes minutes, not seconds. Requiring a split to complete in one go means that a crash after the first 10 000 F has been physically taken leaves the operator with money and no record — the worst possible failure for trust. Guardrail: a ticket resumed with captured tenders cannot have its lines edited; the total is already frozen. A ticket left part-tendered at shift close is surfaced in the close flow as a required resolution (see 20.6).

21.3 · Discount threshold — per tenant, in two numbers, defaulted low

Decision: two tenant-level settings — a line discount ceiling (default 10 %) and a ticket discount ceiling (default 5 % or 5 000 F, whichever is lower). Under the ceiling: cashier acts freely. Over it: supervisor approval on the spot, with a reason picked from a short list. Price override is always above the line — never a cashier action.

Why: the threshold must be per tenant, because a hardware shop that negotiates every sale and a pharmacy that never discounts cannot share a number. It must not be per role, which would make a role change silently a pricing change and make audit impossible to reason about. Defaults are deliberately tight: discount leakage by cashiers is the most common margin loss in small West African retail, and it is far easier to loosen a limit than to recover money already given away. Guardrail: approvals are logged with both operators — who asked, who approved — and the shift report shows discount total as a percentage of net sales, which is the number an owner can actually act on.

21.4 · Offline ticket numbering — register prefix, never renumber

Decision: every ticket number is composed at the till as LOC-REG-SESSION-SEQ (e.g. DLA1-C2-0914-0037), so collisions are structurally impossible and the server never renumbers. The server assigns a separate internal id for its own use.

Why: the printed number is the customer's claim on a return and the auditor's trail. If the server can renumber, a receipt in a customer's pocket stops matching the record — an unrecoverable support problem, and in a tax context, a real risk. A prefix costs nothing and needs no coordination, which is exactly right for tills that spend hours offline. Guardrail: the number is generated once and is immutable thereafter. Genuine duplicates (a restored device backup, say) are quarantined for a human to resolve, never silently merged.

21.5 · Mobile-money reference — system-generated, never typed

Decision: Koomzo always generates the reference itself. The operator is never asked to type a transaction id. Three modes, in order of preference:

1Push (integrated). The register sends a payment request to the customer's number; they approve on their handset. The tender is Paid only on a confirmed success from the provider. Nothing to type, nothing to trust.
2Attest (no integration yet). The customer pays the merchant code themselves and the operator confirms receipt against their own MoMo SMS. Koomzo records an attested tender — operator, amount, wallet, time — and optionally the last 4–6 digits of the customer's number. The last-4 field is optional; a full transaction id is not requested, because a hurried cashier will mistype it and a mistyped reference is worse than none.
3Manual. Recorded like cash-out-of-drawer, reconciled from the wallet statement at close.

Why: the question assumed a reference is something the operator supplies. On this rail it isn't — the initiating system owns the reference and the provider's id arrives with the confirmation. Asking for typed input adds keystrokes at the queue's worst moment and produces data nobody can reconcile. Guardrail: attested tenders are visibly marked as attested, are counted separately in the Z report, and are the tender type most worth a daily owner review — because attestation is, by construction, a statement of trust in the cashier.

21.6 · Unresolved holds at close — block, with one supervised escape

Decision: the Z close lists every held or part-tendered ticket and will not complete until each is settled, voided, or transferred to the next shift. Bulk auto-void exists but is supervisor-only and writes a reason against every ticket it touches.

Why: a held ticket is either goods off the shelf or food already eaten. Auto-voiding silently turns unexplained shrinkage into a clean report, which is precisely the report an owner should not receive. Blocking makes the cashier resolve it while they still remember what happened — the only moment the information exists. Guardrail: transfer-to-next-shift keeps hospitality workable (a table still eating at shift change) and reassigns ownership explicitly, so the ticket always has exactly one responsible operator.

21.7 · Station routing beyond hospitality — one model, hidden by default

Decision: keep the single station-and-course routing model; expose it to non-hospitality trades as a capability called Prepare, off by default. When on, a line whose product is marked prepared gets a prep ticket routed to its station; "prepare" is simply routing with one station and no courses.

Why: a separate "prepare" flag would be a second fulfilment model to build, test and explain, and the moment a bakery has both an oven and a juice counter, it needs the real one anyway. Hiding it by default protects the shop that sells only packaged goods from ever seeing the concept. Guardrail: the copy adapts to the trade — a salon sees "Send to stylist", a butcher "Send to counter" — but the object underneath is one kitchen ticket, so the production display is built once.
