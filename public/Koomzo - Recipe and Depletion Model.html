Design note · Restaurant & bar stock

Recipes, pours, and the depletion ladder

The worry was right: "define a recipe for every meal" is how restaurant inventory projects die. A chef will not enter 14 grams of bouillon cube per plate, and if the ladder starts there, nobody climbs it. But the answer is not to skip recipes — it is to make four separate products out of what other vendors sell as one, and let a venue stop at whichever rung pays for itself. Two useful facts shorten the work: Koomzo already has the recipe primitive (type:'composite' in Inventory, MR.explode() in MRP), and in a West/Central African bar the large majority of revenue is bottled — beer, water, soft drinks — which needs no recipe at all.

1 · The short answer

Do not ship "restaurant recipes" as a feature. Ship a depletion ladder with four rungs, each independently switchable per item, each paying for itself before the next is offered:

RungWhat it isSetup cost to the venueTypical share of a Douala bar-restaurant's revenue
0No stock linkNone — the defaultEverything, on day one
1Direct — menu line is a stock itemOne toggle per item. Beer, water, soft drinks, cigarettes, bottled wine~60–75% of bar revenue, and it is the stock that walks
2Recipe — a line depletes components2–5 minutes per drink or dish, onceCocktails, spirits by the dose, the 10–15 highest-volume plates
3Prep batch — make once, sell by the glassOne recipe + a morning "made 20 L" entryBissap, folleré, ginger, sauces, marinades
4Variance — counted vs expectedA physical count, twiceNothing directly; it is the report that justifies rungs 1–3

The progressive-disclosure claim. A venue that never leaves rung 1 still gets the single most valuable thing restaurant inventory offers in this market: it knows how many beers it should have. That is a complete, shippable product. Rungs 2–4 are opt-in depth, surfaced only once rung 1 is in use.

2 · What Koomzo already has (more than expected)

This is not a greenfield feature. Three of the four pieces exist:

PieceWhereState
Recipe primitiveinventory/iv-data.jsExists. type:'composite' with recipe:[{id,qty}]. The composite capability is already described as "A sale depletes components, not the parent."
Recipe editor UIinventory/iv-items.jsxExists. A Recipe tab on composite items, with component cost roll-up and a Buildable now figure — already the right shape for a bar.
Multi-level explodemrp/mrp-data.jsExists. MR.explode() recurses 4 deep, handles yield < 100%, co-products, rolled cost. Rung 3 is this, renamed.
Movement ledgerkz-stock.jsExists as platform, with kz-stock-sales.js posting on paid for five modules.
Restaurant ↔ Inventory linkrestaurant/rdata.jsMissing. RK_MENU is a standalone catalogue — {id,cat,name,price,station,tags}. No item id, no unit, no recipe, no stock field. This is the entire gap.

So the work is not "build a recipe system." It is: give a menu line an optional pointer into Inventory, and let that pointer resolve through machinery that already ships. That reframing is what keeps this small.

3 · What the other vendors do — and what to take

Four systems, four different bets on where to put the complexity.

Odoo

Uses its manufacturing Kit BOM: Kit-type bills of material generate ingredient stock moves at the point of sale with no manufacturing order in between1, while genuinely pre-made batches use a normal Manufacture BOM created in advance2. Take: the two-mode split — per-sale explosion vs. pre-made batch — is exactly rungs 2 and 3. Leave: routing a restaurant through a Manufacturing app.

Toast

Splits the product in two. The POS itself only does menu-item countdown and 86-ing — "It knows nothing about ingredients, vendors, or ordering"3. Real recipes, counts and actual-vs-theoretical live in a separate paid back-office tier4, where theoretical consumption is ingredient amount in a recipe × items sold5. Prep items deplete as themselves, not as their raw components6. Take: the tier split, and the prep-item rule — it is rung 3 in one sentence. Leave: the 2–3 day reporting lag7.

Loyverse

The small-venue benchmark, and the closest to Koomzo's buyer. Its bar story is deliberately shallow: items you buy and sell are tracked directly; composed drinks need a simple ingredient list, and there is no variance engine. Take: the honesty of stopping early. Most of its bars only ever track bottles.

Lightspeed

Retail-first, with ingredient-level tracking positioned as an upper-tier / add-on concern rather than a day-one setup task. Take: never gate the register on inventory depth being configured.

The consensus none of them state outright: every one of these systems lets an operator sell profitably with zero recipes configured, and every one puts recipe depth behind a later tier. Koomzo's differentiator is not having recipes — it is making rung 1 so cheap to adopt (one toggle, no new screen) that a 6-table bar actually uses it.

4 · The depletion ladder

One field on a menu line decides everything: stock. Absent = rung 0. The register never changes behaviour; only what the adapter posts changes.

// restaurant/rdata.js — the only schema change to RK_MENU { id:'be1', name:'33 Export 65cl', price:1000, station:'bar', stock:{ mode:'direct', item:'bv12', qty:1 } } // rung 1 { id:'co1', name:'Bissap Rhum', price:2500, station:'bar', stock:{ mode:'recipe', lines:[ { item:'sp-rum', qty:4, unit:'cl' }, { item:'pr-bis', qty:12, unit:'cl' }, // a prep item — rung 3 { item:'gn-ice', qty:1, unit:'scoop' } ] } } // rung 2 { id:'mn2', name:'Poulet DG', price:4500, station:'grill', stock:{ mode:'recipe', lines:[ { item:'fd-chk', qty:0.35, unit:'kg' }, { item:'fd-pla', qty:2, unit:'pc' } ] } }
0
Off — the menu is a price listDefault · all tierssetup: none

No stock key. The line sells, prints to its station, and posts nothing. This must stay the default forever — a new venue configures zero inventory to open its first ticket, and today's Restaurant module behaves exactly this way, so rung 0 is already shipped and already correct.

86 / "rupture" still works here. A manual out-of-stock toggle per menu item is a service tool, not an inventory feature, and belongs at rung 0. It is the single most-requested thing in a market where the fish finishes at 8pm.

1
Direct — the menu line is a stock itemCore · with Inventorysetup: one toggle

Sell one 33 Export 65cl, one bottle leaves stock. No recipe, no unit maths, no new concept. In Inventory this is an ordinary type:'product' item that gained pos:true — the mechanism Products v2 already uses to put an item on a register tile.

Setup UX: in the menu-item sheet, a single row — Track stock → item picker, pre-filtered to items whose name fuzzy-matches the menu line. For a drinks list this is a 20-item, five-minute job, and a Match all button should do most of it: bar catalogues and supplier invoices use the same names.

Covers: beer, soft drinks, water, bottled wine, cigarettes, packaged snacks, sachets. In a typical bar-restaurant this is the majority of units sold and nearly all of the shrinkage risk.

2
Recipe — one line depletes several componentsPlussetup: 2–5 min per item

The existing composite capability, reached from the menu item instead of the Inventory item. A cocktail is 4cl rum + 12cl bissap + 1 lime wedge; a plate is 0.35kg chicken + 2 plantain.

The bar case is the easy one and should lead the design. Spirits are poured in fixed doses — 4cl, 2cl for a shot — so a bottle is unit:'cl', on-hand is 70cl per bottle, and the pour is one number. This is genuinely accurate. Food is the hard one: portions vary, and a venue that enters 0.35kg of chicken will find its variance report is mostly noise. So:

Default food recipes to the "countable" components only. When the operator adds a recipe to Poulet DG, pre-suggest the things that come in units — the chicken, the plantain — and leave oil, salt, bouillon and spice out entirely, with one line of copy: "Track what you count. Leave out oil, salt and spices — they add work, not accuracy." Toast's variance model rests on recipe accuracy5; a half-recipe that is right beats a full recipe that is invented.

Reuse, do not rebuild: the Recipe tab in iv-items.jsx is the editor. The menu sheet links to it. "Buildable now" becomes the bar's "how many mojitos are left in the house" number — already computed there as min(floor(onHand / qty)).

3
Prep batch — make 20 L in the morning, sell by the glassPlussetup: recipe + a daily entry

Bissap, folleré, ginger juice, palm-nut base, marinade, piment sauce, pounded cocoyam. An intermediate item with its own recipe and its own on-hand, made in a batch, then consumed by drinks and dishes. This is Odoo's pre-made-batch BOM2 and Toast's prep item — where the system depletes the stock of the prep item itself, rather than its individual raw components6.

That rule matters and should be copied verbatim: selling a glass of bissap depletes bissap, not hibiscus and sugar. Raw components are consumed once, at Make time, by the person who made the batch. It keeps the sale one level deep and the maths obvious.

The whole UI is one button. On a prep item: Make batch → yield quantity → post. That is the entire manufacturing story a restaurant needs; MR.explode() already does the component side, including yield loss. No work orders, no routings, no MRP app.

Spoilage. Prep items expire — a batch of bissap is a two-day item. Depth beyond a Discard rest button (posting waste for the remainder at close) is out of scope for v1, but the button is not: without it, on-hand drifts upward forever and every rung above it becomes untrustworthy.

4
Variance — counted vs expectedPro / latersetup: two physical counts

The report that pays for rungs 1–3: expected on-hand from the ledger, actual from a count, difference attributed to waste, over-pour or theft. Toast requires two completed counts plus product-mix mapping before it will show the analysis8 — a genuine constraint, not a limitation to design around.

Keep it out of v1, but do one thing now: make the count screen and the movement ledger record enough to compute it later. Specifically, every posted movement needs source (sale · fire · make · waste · count) so the variance query is a grouping, not an archaeology project.

For a bar, the one number worth previewing early is per-bottle: expected 12 doses, poured 9. That is the conversation an owner actually wants to have with a barman.

5 · When stock moves — and the split that is already in the code

The fire-vs-pay question resolves cleanly once you notice that register.jsx already excludes bar items from firing: both fireCourse() and sendAll() filter on station !== 'bar'. Drinks never go to the kitchen. So the timing rule writes itself along a line the code already draws:

WhatPosts whenAdapterWhy
Kitchen item
station ≠ bar		Firekz-stock-fire.js newFood is consumed when it is cooked. A table that walks out still ate the chicken. Fire time is also when the kitchen can react to a shortage.
Bar item
station = bar		Paidkz-stock-sales.js existsBar items never fire — there is no fire event to hook. They behave exactly like a Retail Flex line, so they use the adapter that already ships.
Voided after fireVoidfire adapterPosts kind:'waste', never a reversal. The food was made and thrown away — that is the number the owner needs to see.
Cancelled before fire——Nothing was consumed. Nothing posts.
Prep batch madeMakekz-stock.jsComponents out, prep item in, at the moment the cook says so.

This lands on top of a live defect. clearTable() (store.jsx:97) deletes every non-done ticket for a table, and finishOrder() calls it on payment — so food still cooking silently disappears. Once fire posts stock, that deletion becomes a stock bug as well as a service bug: consumed food with no record of where it went. Fix the ticket lifecycle first (cancel with a reason, not delete), then wire the adapter.

6 · Units, pours and the one conversion we allow

Unit conversion is where recipe systems bloat. Koomzo should permit exactly one level: a purchase unit and a use unit, with an integer or simple decimal factor set once on the item.

Not this
density tables · custom UoM classes · per-recipe overrides · yield % on every line · nested conversions
This
Rum buy: bottle 70cl use: cl Chicken buy: kg use: kg Plantain buy: bunch (12) use: pc Bissap buy: — make: L use: cl

On-hand displays in the purchase unit (8 bottles), because that is how it is counted and ordered; recipes are written in the use unit (4cl), because that is how it is poured. The register shows neither. iv-data.js already carries unit per item with 'ml', 'sheet', 'pack' in use — the shape exists; it needs the second field and the factor.

7 · Modifiers — the trap to avoid in v1

"Extra cheese", "double shot", "no piment" each theoretically adjust a recipe. Odoo users hit this immediately9. Full modifier-to-recipe mapping multiplies setup cost by the number of modifiers and is the single fastest way to make rung 2 unaffordable.

v1 rule: a modifier may carry its own direct stock line (extra cheese = 1 × cheese slice) but may not edit the parent recipe. A "double" modifier multiplies only the lines that name the spirit. Anything more expressive waits for evidence that venues want it.

8 · The setup story — where progressive disclosure actually lives

Rungs are worthless if the operator meets them all at once. The disclosure is in the sequence, not the feature list.

Day 1 — open the tillrung 0

Menu, prices, stations, tickets. Inventory is not mentioned. Nothing about stock appears anywhere in Restaurant.

Day 8 — Inventory is switched onrung 1

One card appears on the Restaurant settings screen: "Track your drinks stock — 24 of your menu lines look like items you buy. Match them?" One screen, two columns, a Match-all button, done in five minutes. No recipe concept is introduced.

Week 4 — the first countrung 1 → value

Counted 84 beers, expected 91. The seven-bottle gap is the entire business case, and it arrives before the operator has written a single recipe. This is the moment the product earns rung 2, and only here does the recipe prompt appear: "Your cocktails aren't tracked. Add a recipe to the five you sell most?"

Month 2+ — depth, on requestrungs 2–3

Recipes on the top sellers. Prep batches when someone asks why bissap never runs out on paper. Food recipes last, on the 10–15 highest-volume plates — never as a "complete the menu" task, and never with a completeness meter, which would turn a good half-configured system into a guilt generator.

Never show an unconfigured venue a recipe field. The Recipe tab, the prep-item type and the variance report are all hidden until the rung below them is in active use — the same capability-gating kz-caps.js already performs for module tiers.

12 · Your call

1
Is the ladder the right shape — four rungs, rung 1 in Lite, recipes at Plus, variance at Pro? The alternative is recipes-or-nothing, which is what the market does and what you were right to be wary of.
2
Fire-time for food, pay-time for bar — confirmed? It follows the station !== 'bar' line already in the code, so it costs nothing structurally, but it means a walked table still shows consumption.
3
Post-fire void posts waste, not a reversal. This is the accounting-honest option and makes waste visible. Confirm you want the number visible — some owners would rather not see it.
4
Ticket lifecycle: cancel-with-reason replacing delete. Needed for stock integrity, but it changes what the kitchen sees during service — cancelled cards persist until acknowledged. Acceptable?
5
Ship at step 6 and stop? My recommendation. Rungs 2–3 are designed and cheap once 1–6 land, but building them before a venue asks risks a feature nobody configures.
6
Does this go into the Ionic port as-is, or does the React source get fixed first? Steps 1–2 are defects that exist in both codebases; steps 3–10 are new work that arguably should only be built once, in Angular.
